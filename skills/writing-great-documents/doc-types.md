# Document Types

One document, one type. Each type below gives **when to use it** and its **shape** — the section skeleton. Fill the skeleton; drop sections the reader's job doesn't need.

## Technical: the four Diátaxis modes

Keep these four apart — a page that teaches *and* lists reference *and* argues is three documents fighting. Pick by what the reader is doing.

### Tutorial — learning by doing
When: the reader is new and needs a guided first success.
Shape: what you'll build → prerequisites → numbered steps that always work → what you learned → next step. Concrete over complete; no detours into why.

### How-to — steps to a goal
When: the reader knows the basics and has a specific task.
Shape: the goal in one line → prerequisites → numbered steps → how to verify it worked. Assume competence; link to reference instead of explaining it inline.

### Reference — lookup facts
When: the reader needs to look something up, not read top to bottom.
Shape: a consistent entry per item (name, signature/params, returns, errors, one example). Grouped or alphabetical; accurate and exhaustive; no narrative.

### Explanation — why and background
When: the reader wants to understand a concept, decision, or trade-off.
Shape: the question → the context → the discussion (alternatives, why this one) → implications. Prose, not steps.

## Non-technical

### Decision memo
When: a decision is needed, or has been made and must be recorded.
Shape: **the decision up front** → context and problem → options considered → rationale → implications and next steps.

### Proposal
When: you want a reader to approve, fund, or commit to something.
Shape: the ask up front → the problem and why it matters now → the proposed approach → cost/effort and alternatives → exactly what you need from the reader.

### Status update
When: stakeholders need to know where things stand.
Shape: headline (on track / at risk / blocked) → progress since last update → what's next → risks and asks. Skimmable; no archaeology.

### Narrative
When: you are explaining or persuading at length (essay, long doc, announcement body).
Shape: hook → one central claim → ordered supporting points → conclusion. One claim per document.

### Announcement / email
When: you need a reader to read and act fast.
Shape: a subject/headline that states the point → BLUF first line → the one action you want → details below the fold. Shorter is more likely to be read.
